  The larger issue is that JoukowskiSim spends only about 9% of each step in FFT/DCT operations. It performs
  roughly 3,081 small Poisson/radial/Thom LU solves per step, which dominate runtime. MLX 0.32.2 currently reports
  that lu_factor and solve_triangular are unsupported on Metal, despite appearing in the general API.

  Therefore an FFT-only patch would provide little whole-solver improvement and convert float64 state to float32;
  MLX documents that GPU float64 is unsupported and NumPy float64 converts to MLX float32. MLX data types
  (https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/python/data_types.html), NumPy conversion
  (https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/usage/numpy.html)

  The worthwhile route is:

  - Keep the SciPy float64 solver as the reference.
  - Add a persistent MLX backend for FFTs, derivatives, nonlinear products, state and RK stages.
  - Implement a custom Metal batched LU-apply kernel for the cached Poisson/radial factors.
  - Transfer to NumPy only for rendered/pressure frames.
  - Validate Cp, circulation, wall slip and residuals against float64.

  That can deliver a meaningful acceleration. An FFT-only switch cannot reproduce the turbo solver’s 187 FPS.
